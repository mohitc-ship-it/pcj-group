## PART 1: COMPLETE SEAM DICTIONARY

---

### 1. PLAIN SEAM
**Industry Code:** PS  
**Description:** Two fabric pieces joined right-sides together, stitched with a single row of stitching, and pressed open or to one side.

**When to Use:**
- Foundation seam for most woven garments.
- Woven dress bodices, blouses, trousers (when another finish is applied on top).
- Always used as a base before another edge finish (serging, pinking, binding) is applied.
- Standard for most non-stress seams in structured garments.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- On the right side of the garment, you see a clean, invisible join with no visible stitching.
- On the inside, you see two fabric edges folded away from each other (pressed open) OR to one side, usually with a separate overlock or pinked edge.
- No visible topstitching on the exterior.

---

### 2. FRENCH SEAM
**Industry Code:** FS  
**Description:** A seam encased within itself — sewn wrong-sides together first, then right-sides together so the raw edges are fully hidden inside.

**When to Use:**
- **Sheer/Lightweight fabrics:** Chiffon, organza, voile, silk georgette — where the seam allowance would show through the fabric.
- **Luxury lingerie and nightwear.**
- **Baby clothing** — soft, no-scratch interior.
- Curved seams on lightweight garments (side seams, shoulder seams of sheer blouses).
- NOT suitable for thick or non-pliable fabrics.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- From the outside: A clean, slightly raised ridge/ridge line. No raw edges visible at all, even on the inside.
- From the inside: You see a narrow, enclosed tube of fabric — both fabric edges are completely hidden. The seam has a thin, neat, rounded look with no fraying edges visible anywhere.
- **Key Tell:** If you look at the inside and see a completely enclosed seam with NO raw edges at all — it's a French seam.

---

### 3. FLAT-FELLED SEAM (Run-and-Fell)
**Industry Code:** FFS  
**Description:** One seam allowance is trimmed, the other is folded over it and topstitched flat, creating two parallel rows of topstitching on the right side.

**When to Use:**
- **Denim jeans, jackets, and jeanswear** — the standard seam on all side seams and inseams.
- **Casual shirts (men's dress shirts)** — shoulder seams and sleeve seams.
- **Workwear, outerwear** — where extreme durability and strength is needed.
- **Sportswear and activewear** — seams that get heavy stress.
- Any garment that needs zero exposed raw edges AND high durability.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- **KEY IDENTIFIER: Two parallel rows of visible topstitching on the right side of the fabric.** This is unmistakable.
- The two rows of stitching are usually 3–6mm apart.
- You will always see this on the outer legs of jeans, side seams of casual shirts.
- On the inside, the seam allowance is folded flat and caught down — no raw edges visible.
- **If you see two parallel stitch lines on a seam, it is almost certainly a flat-felled seam.**

---

### 4. MOCK FLAT-FELLED SEAM (Lapped Seam)
**Industry Code:** MFS  
**Description:** Mimics the look of a flat-felled seam on the outside (two rows of topstitching) but is constructed faster — no trimming is required, with raw edges secured by an overlocker on the inside.

**When to Use:**
- **Mass-production garments** where the flat-felled look is desired but speed is essential.
- **Ready-to-wear jeans and casualwear** — a cost-effective alternative to true flat-felled.
- **Activewear** — provides the look without the labor-intensive construction.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- From the outside: Identical to flat-felled — two parallel rows of stitching.
- **From the inside: The raw edges are overlocked, NOT neatly folded over.** This is how you distinguish it from a true flat-felled seam.

---

### 5. OVERLOCK / SERGED SEAM (Safety Stitch)
**Industry Code:** OLS  
**Description:** A combination stitch that sews the seam, trims the seam allowance, and encases the raw edge simultaneously using a serger/overlocker machine.

**When to Use:**
- **Knitwear** — T-shirts, jersey dresses, leggings, any stretch fabric. This is the standard seam for all knit garments.
- **Fast fashion and mass production** for speed and cost.
- **Casual woven garments** — as an edge finish on plain seams.
- Any garment requiring a degree of stretch at the seam.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- From the outside: A clean seam line, sometimes slightly raised.
- From the inside: **A chain of looped thread that wraps around and catches the raw edge of the fabric.** The edge looks like it's been "wrapped" in thread, like an interlocking chain. Very distinctive.
- You can see the looping thread clearly on the fabric edge on the inside.
- Found on virtually every T-shirt ever made.

---

### 6. BOUND SEAM (Hong Kong Finish)
**Industry Code:** BS  
**Description:** The raw seam allowances are individually wrapped/bound in a separate strip of fabric (usually bias-cut) or a pre-made tape, then pressed.

**When to Use:**
- **Unlined, high-end tailoring** — blazers, jackets, coats where the inside of the garment is fully visible.
- **Couture and luxury ready-to-wear** — the mark of quality, expensive construction.
- **Unlined jackets** — where the interior finish must be beautiful.
- Fabrics that fray excessively (tweeds, boucles, loosely woven).
- NOT used on garments that are fully lined (since the lining covers the seams).

**How to Identify Visually:**
- On the inside of the garment, each seam allowance edge is neatly **wrapped in a strip of fabric** — usually a coordinating or contrasting fabric in a bias-cut strip.
- The bound edge is clean, flat, and looks almost decorative.
- **Key Tell:** If you open the jacket and see beautifully bound edges (often in silk or a contrasting color), this is the Hong Kong/Bound finish. It is a luxury quality indicator.

---

### 7. WELT SEAM (Single or Double)
**Industry Code:** WS  
**Description:** A seam where one or both of the seam allowances is turned up and topstitched down toward the seam line, creating a raised "welt" effect.

**When to Use:**
- **Tailoring** — used decoratively on blazer sleeves, coat seams.
- **Leather and faux leather** garments — where a plain seam would be too bulky.
- **Sportswear and activewear** — for a decorative and functional construction.
- **Suede and napped fabrics** — avoids the bulk of a pressed-open seam.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- One or two rows of topstitching visible, but VERY close to the seam line (unlike flat-felled, the seam edge isn't folded completely over).
- A raised, slightly padded ridge is visible along the seam from the outside.

---

### 8. PRINCESS SEAM
**Industry Code:** PRS  
**Description:** Not technically a "seam type" but a **seam placement** — a long, curved vertical seam that runs from the shoulder or armhole down through the bust and to the hem, creating shaping without darts.

**When to Use:**
- **Women's fitted bodices and dresses** — provides bust, waist, and hip shaping simultaneously.
- **Evening gowns, formal wear, structured dresses.**
- **Fitted jackets and coats** — instead of bust darts.
- Anytime a garment needs to be precisely fitted to the body's curves.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- A long, vertical seam that curves around the bust. It flows continuously from the shoulder or armhole down the front of the garment.
- The seam curves OUTWARD around the fullest part of the bust, then curves BACK IN at the waist.
- Found on the front and/or back panels of fitted garments.

---

### 9. LAPPED SEAM
**Industry Code:** LS  
**Description:** One fabric piece overlaps the other, with stitching done through both layers on the top piece.

**When to Use:**
- **Yoke constructions** — the yoke laps over the shirt body.
- **Waistbands** — overlap stitched down.
- **Leather and denim applications** — where a clean, non-bulky join is needed.
- **Decorative applications** where the overlap is intentional and visible.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- One piece of fabric visibly sits on top of the other.
- One or two rows of topstitching holding the top layer down.
- Common on shirt yoke-to-body joins.

---

### 10. STAY-STITCHING (Not a Seam, but a Construction Requirement)
**Industry Code:** SS  
**Description:** A single row of stitching placed WITHIN the seam allowance (not crossing it) on curved or bias-cut edges to prevent them from stretching out during construction.

**When to Use:**
- **Necklines** — curved necklines stretch easily without it.
- **Armholes** — on the bodice, before attaching the sleeve.
- **Any curved cut edge** before assembly.
- Performed BEFORE the garment is assembled, not visible in the finished garment.

---

### 11. TOPSTITCHING
**Industry Code:** TS  
**Description:** Decorative or functional stitching visible on the right side (outside) of the garment, usually parallel to a seam or edge.

**When to Use:**
- **To reinforce seams** — collar seams, waistbands, pockets.
- **To flatten and hold seam allowances** — prevents seam roll.
- **Decorative purposes** — contrast thread on denim, colored topstitching.
- Virtually all casual and sportswear garments.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- A row (or rows) of stitching clearly visible from the right side, parallel to a seam or edge.
- Can be done in matching thread (less visible) or contrasting thread (very visible — denim yellow topstitching is the classic example).

---

### 12. EDGE STITCH
**Industry Code:** ES  
**Description:** A very precise row of topstitching placed 1–2mm from a folded edge or seam.

**When to Use:**
- **Collars and cuffs** — to give a sharp, crisp edge.
- **Pocket openings and flaps** — to reinforce and define.
- **Button plackets** — to keep them flat and sharp.
- Any edge that needs to stay perfectly crisp and flat.

**How to Identify Visually:**
- A row of stitching running extremely close to the fabric edge (visually looks like it's almost on the very edge itself).
- Often paired with topstitching — edge stitch is the inner row, topstitching is the outer row.

## PART 2: FABRIC-TO-SEAM PAIRING QUICK REFERENCE TABLE

| Fabric Type | Recommended Seam | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Denim (heavy) | Flat-Felled | Maximum durability, industry standard |
| Chiffon / Organza | French Seam | Hides raw edges, no show-through |
| Jersey Knit (T-shirt) | Overlock/Serge | Allows stretch, fast production |
| Silk | French Seam | Prevents fraying, clean finish |
| Wool / Tweed (unlined) | Bound / Hong Kong | Prevents fraying, high-end finish |
| Cotton Poplin (shirt) | Flat-Felled or Plain+Serge | Clean, durable for casual wear |
| Faux Leather / PU | Welt or Lapped | Avoids bulk, can't be ironed |
| Stretch Velvet | Overlock | Handles stretch |
| Lace (sheer) | French Seam | Invisible finish |
| Structured Wool (blazer, lined) | Plain + Bound | Interior quality |
| Activewear / Lycra | Overlock (3-thread) or Flatlock | Full stretch recovery |

## PART 3: FEW-SHOT EXAMPLES FOR AI PROMPT

---

**EXAMPLE 1:**
- **Input:** A sheer silk chiffon blouse. Side seams and shoulder seams visible. The inside of the garment shows no raw edges anywhere.
- **AI Output should be:**
  - Seam Type: French Seam
  - Application: Side seams, shoulder seams
  - Justification: Fabric is sheer (chiffon/silk). French seams are mandatory to prevent raw edges from showing through the fabric. The image confirms no raw edges inside, which is characteristic of French seam construction.

---

**EXAMPLE 2:**
- **Input:** A pair of medium-wash denim jeans. The outer side seam has two clearly visible parallel rows of yellow topstitching approximately 5mm apart.
- **AI Output should be:**
  - Seam Type: Flat-Felled Seam
  - Application: Outer side seam, inseam
  - Justification: Two parallel rows of visible topstitching is the defining characteristic of a flat-felled seam. Standard and mandatory construction for denim jeans. The yellow thread confirms topstitching.

---

**EXAMPLE 3:**
- **Input:** A cropped jersey T-shirt. Inside seams show an interlocking chain-like thread wrapping around the fabric edge. Fabric has visible stretch.
- **AI Output should be:**
  - Seam Type: 4-Thread Overlock (Safety Stitch)
  - Application: Side seams, shoulder seams
  - Justification: The interlocking chain thread on the inside is the unmistakable signature of an overlock stitch. This is standard for all knit/jersey garments as it allows the seam to stretch with the fabric.

---

**EXAMPLE 4:**
- **Input:** An unlined blazer in a houndstooth wool fabric. The inside of the blazer shows seam allowances wrapped in a silk bias-cut strip.
- **AI Output should be:**
  - Seam Type: Bound Seam (Hong Kong Finish)
  - Application: All internal seams
  - Justification: Houndstooth wool frays easily. Since the blazer is unlined, raw edges must be finished. The bias-cut silk binding wrapped around each seam allowance is the Hong Kong finish, a quality indicator in unlined tailored garments.

---

**EXAMPLE 5:**
- **Input:** A women's fitted cocktail dress with long vertical curved seams running from the armhole down around the bust to the hem. No darts present.
- **AI Output should be:**
  - Seam Type: Princess Seam (constructed using Plain Seam + Serged edge inside)
  - Application: Front and back vertical body panels
  - Justification: The long, uninterrupted curved vertical seam bypassing the bust area with no darts present is the defining characteristic of a princess seam. Provides 3D shaping without darts, standard for fitted formal dresses.

---

**EXAMPLE 6:**
- **Input:** A men's classic dress shirt (Oxford weave cotton). The shoulder seam and sleeve attachment seam both have two parallel rows of topstitching from the outside.
- **AI Output should be:**
  - Seam Type: Flat-Felled Seam
  - Application: Shoulder seams, sleeve seams
  - Justification: Two parallel rows of topstitching confirm a flat-felled seam. This is standard construction for men's dress shirts, providing both durability and a clean inside finish.

## PART 4: KEY VISUAL IDENTIFICATION RULES (FOR AI)

```
RULE 1 — TWO PARALLEL STITCH LINES = FLAT-FELLED (or Mock Flat-Felled)
  If you see two parallel rows of topstitching on the outside of a seam → ALWAYS call it Flat-Felled or Mock Flat-Felled seam.

RULE 2 — NO RAW EDGES INSIDE, ENCLOSED SEAM = FRENCH SEAM
  If the inside of the garment shows a neat enclosed ridge with ZERO raw edges visible → French Seam.

RULE 3 — CHAIN-LOOPED THREAD ON INSIDE EDGE = OVERLOCK
  If the inside edge shows interlocked looping chain-thread wrapping around the fabric edge → Overlock/Serge seam.

RULE 4 — BIAS STRIP WRAPPING INSIDE EDGES = BOUND / HONG KONG
  If seam allowances have a separate strip of fabric bound around them on the inside → Bound (Hong Kong) seam.

RULE 5 — SHEER FABRIC = FRENCH SEAM (MANDATORY)
  If fabric is described as sheer, chiffon, organza, voile, silk georgette → Default to French Seam unless explicitly overridden.

RULE 6 — JERSEY/KNIT/STRETCH FABRIC = OVERLOCK (MANDATORY)
  If fabric is jersey, knit, lycra, spandex, ponte, rib knit → Default to Overlock/Serge seam.

RULE 7 — DENIM = FLAT-FELLED (MANDATORY)
  If garment is denim jeans, denim jacket → Default to Flat-Felled seam on side, inseam.

RULE 8 — UNLINED LUXURY JACKET = BOUND SEAM
  If garment is a blazer/coat/jacket that is unlined → Bound/Hong Kong seam on internal seams.

RULE 9 — LEATHER/FAUX LEATHER = WELT OR LAPPED
  If fabric is leather, faux leather, PU, or suede → Welt or Lapped seam (cannot press open a plain seam on leather).

RULE 10 — FITTED BODICE, NO DARTS = PRINCESS SEAM
  If a women's fitted bodice has long vertical curved seams but no bust darts → Princess Seam construction.
```
